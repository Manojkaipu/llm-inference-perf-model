# Bottleneck map for Llama-style inference on T4 and H100-SXM

## Summary

- **On the T4 (calibrated profile), decode batching stops paying off early.** For TinyLlama, the weight matmuls become compute-bound at batch 64 (context 2048), because the fitted compute efficiency (0.2566) is much lower than the memory efficiency (0.8533). Going from batch 64 to 256 at context 512 only raises throughput from 3,047 to 4,629 tok/s. With the datasheet peak profile the same transition would sit at batch 235.
- **KV-cache reads take over decode sooner than a pure roofline predicts.** On the calibrated T4, TinyLlama decode at batch 1 becomes attention-dominated at a context of **21,548**. With uncalibrated profiles (T4 prior and H100 prior) the crossover is at 91,887. The calibrated attention kernel runs well below the bandwidth roof: 7.87 ms against 1.52 ms at peak, for the same operation.
- **On the H100 (prior profile), small models are limited by kernel launch overhead at small batch.** Overhead is 52% of a TinyLlama decode step at batch 1. It only falls below 10% at batch 928 (context 512). This result rests entirely on the uncalibrated 3 µs-per-kernel prior.
- **Memory capacity sets hard limits.** Llama-3.1-8B in FP16 does not fit on the T4 at all: 16.06 GB is needed against a usable 14.4 GB, even at context 1. On the H100 the 8B model reaches its memory limit at batch 209 (context 2048), before its weight matmuls ever become compute-bound (that would happen at batch 264).
- **Prefill becomes compute-bound at short prompts.** This happens at 68–81 tokens on the T4 (calibrated) and 284–364 tokens on the H100 (prior). Attention overtakes the weight matmuls at prompt lengths of about 21k (TinyLlama), 29k (Llama-3.2-1B) and 53k (Llama-3.1-8B), with nearly the same values on both GPUs.

## Profiles used

- **T4: "calibrated"** profile, fitted to llama.cpp measurements of TinyLlama (held-out error about 9% for decode and 4% for prefill). Results for Llama-3.2-1B and Llama-3.1-8B on the T4 are extrapolations.
- **H100-SXM: "prior"** profile, which is hand-set and not measured: compute efficiency 0.7, memory efficiency 0.85, 3 µs per kernel. All H100 numbers are model predictions.
- **"Peak"** and **T4 "prior"** results are used only to isolate where specific assumptions drive a result.

## 1. Decode across batch size

Crossover batch sizes (found with `find_crossover`). The overhead column is at context 512; the other columns are at context 2048.

| GPU (profile) | Model | Overhead <10% | Attention > linear | Linear compute-bound | Memory limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T4 (calibrated) | TinyLlama-1.1B | 91 | 101 | 64 | 265 |
| T4 (calibrated) | Llama-3.2-1B | 39 | 71 | 64 | 178 |
| H100 (prior) | TinyLlama-1.1B | 928 | 47 | 291 | 1,513 |
| H100 (prior) | Llama-3.2-1B | 522 | 38 | 285 | 1,036 |
| H100 (prior) | Llama-3.1-8B | 188 | 57 | 264 (past the memory limit) | 209 |

**How the bottleneck moves as batch grows:**

1. **Launch overhead and weight bandwidth (small batch).** At batch 1 every step reads all the weights once (2.07 GB for TinyLlama). Time is therefore almost independent of batch size: on the T4, 10.42 ms at batch 1 and 10.86 ms at batch 4 (context 512).
   - Per-kernel overhead adds a fixed cost. On the T4 (9.6 µs per kernel) it is 24.7% of the step for TinyLlama and 16.8% for Llama-3.2-1B, which has fewer layers (16 against 22).
   - On the H100 the bandwidth is higher (3,350 against 320 GB/s), so weight reads shrink while the per-kernel cost stays. Overhead becomes the largest component for TinyLlama: 52% at batch 1, and still dominant at batch 64 (43%).
   - As a result, the H100 is predicted to give only 652 against 96 tok/s at batch 1 (context 512), well short of the bandwidth ratio.
2. **KV-cache bandwidth (moderate batch).** Each sequence reads its own KV cache, so attention time grows with batch × context while weight time stays flat. On the H100, attention overtakes the weight matmuls at batch 38–57 (context 2048). At that point the weight matmuls are still memory-bound.
   - The crossover comes later on the T4 (batch 71–101) because linear time there starts growing with batch from batch 64 onward, once the matmuls turn compute-bound.
   - Llama-3.2-1B crosses earlier than TinyLlama on both GPUs because its KV cache is larger per token (32.77 against 22.53 KB; 8 against 4 KV heads).
3. **Compute-bound weight matmuls.** These are shown as memory-bound until the batch reaches the effective ridge point.
   - On the T4 the calibrated profile moves this to batch 64; with the peak profile it is batch 235.
   - On the H100 it happens at batch 285–291 for the 1B models, compared with 368 at peak. By then attention already accounts for most of the step: 72% for TinyLlama at batch 291.
4. **Throughput saturation.** Once attention dominates, adding sequences adds KV reads in proportion, so tokens per second flatten out:

| Case (context 2048) | Smaller batch | Larger batch |
|---|---|---|
| T4 TinyLlama | 2,112 tok/s at batch 64 | 2,804 tok/s at batch 256 |
| H100 TinyLlama | 42,760 tok/s at batch 256 | 48,300 tok/s at batch 1,024 |
| H100 8B | 5,029 tok/s at batch 64 | 7,736 tok/s at batch 208 (memory limit) |

## 2. Decode across context length (batch 1)

| GPU (profile) | Model | Attention > linear at context | Memory limit at context |
|---|---|---|---|
| T4 (calibrated) | TinyLlama-1.1B | 21,548 | 541,544 |
| T4 (calibrated) | Llama-3.2-1B | 28,736 | 364,025 |
| H100 (prior) | TinyLlama-1.1B | 91,887 | not reached up to 1,048,576 |
| H100 (prior) | Llama-3.2-1B | 75,464 | not reached up to 1,048,576 |
| H100 (prior) | Llama-3.1-8B | 114,544 | 426,784 |

**Mechanism.** In the uncalibrated profiles, attention and the weight matmuls run at the same effective bandwidth, so they cross where KV bytes read equal weight bytes read. For TinyLlama on the H100 that is 2.07 GB of KV against 2.069 GB of weights; for the 8B model it is 15.01 GB against 15.01 GB. This explains why the 8B model crosses latest on the H100: its weights are large relative to its 131.1 KB per token of KV cache.

On the calibrated T4, TinyLlama crosses with only 0.49 GB of KV read. The fitted profile captures llama.cpp's decode attention as inefficient: 7.87 ms (calibrated) against 1.52 ms (peak) at context 21,548. The T4 prior profile gives 91,887, the same value as the H100 prior, which confirms that the gap comes from kernel efficiency rather than hardware.

The H100 long-context crossovers therefore assume attention kernels that reach the bandwidth roof. That has not been checked against measurements.

On the T4, Llama-3.2-1B crosses later than TinyLlama, while on the H100 the order is reversed. We did not isolate which model feature interacts with the fitted attention-efficiency term to cause this.

**Memory limits.** Near the limits, decode is almost entirely attention: TinyLlama on the T4 at a context of 541k spends 95% of the step in attention and produces 4.9 tok/s.

## 3. Prefill across prompt length (batch 1)

| GPU (profile) | Model | Linear compute-bound at | Best tok/s in sweep | tok/s at 65,536 | Attention > linear at |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T4 (calibrated) | TinyLlama-1.1B | 68 | 7,096 (1,024) | 2,049 | 21,626 |
| T4 (calibrated) | Llama-3.2-1B | 81 | 7,173 (1,024) | 2,571 | 29,816 |
| H100 (prior) | TinyLlama-1.1B | 311 | 237,500 (4,096) | 82,190 | 21,108 |
| H100 (prior) | Llama-3.2-1B | 364 | 251,600 (4,096) | 102,800 | 29,192 |
| H100 (prior) | Llama-3.1-8B | 284 | 41,300 (1,024) | 21,100 | 52,751 |

**How the bottleneck moves as the prompt grows:**

1. **Short prompts.** These look like decode: one weight read is spread over only a few tokens. On the H100, launch overhead is 52% of a 16-token TinyLlama prefill.
2. **Compute-bound matmuls.** Once the prompt passes the ridge point (68–364 tokens), the weight matmuls are compute-bound and throughput reaches its plateau. The T4 reaches this point earlier for the same reason as in decode: its low calibrated compute efficiency.
3. **Long prompts.** Attention FLOPs grow with the square of the prompt length, while linear FLOPs grow only linearly. Throughput falls, and attention takes over at about 21k, 29k and 53k tokens.
   - These crossovers are nearly the same on the two GPUs (21,626 against 21,108 for TinyLlama) because both components are compute-bound, so the efficiency factors largely cancel out.
   - Models with more weight per layer relative to attention width cross later. The 8B model, with hidden size 4096, crosses latest.
4. **Elementwise operations on the H100.** These are a visible cost: 15.7% of a 4,096-token TinyLlama prefill. On the T4 the corresponding figure is 4.8%.

## 4. Memory capacity

Fit is defined as weights plus KV cache using no more than 90% of GPU memory.

- **Llama-3.1-8B on the T4:** it does not fit in FP16 at any batch size or context length. The tool's throughput figure for it (16.7 tok/s) is hypothetical.
- **Batch limit at context 2048:**
  - T4: 265 (TinyLlama) and 178 (Llama-3.2-1B).
  - H100: 1,513, 1,036 and 209 (8B).
  - The model with the larger KV cache per token always reaches the limit sooner.
- **At context 8192:**
  - On the T4, TinyLlama fits at batch 64 (14.01 GB) but not at 256. Llama-3.2-1B does not fit at batch 64 (19.65 GB).
  - On the H100, the 8B model fits at batch 16 (33.24 GB) but not at 64 (84.79 GB).
- **For the H100 8B model, memory is the binding limit for decode batching.** At context 2048 it runs out of memory (batch 209) before the weight matmuls go compute-bound (batch 264), and its throughput gain is already flattening because attention is 73% of the step at batch 208.

## 5. How the GPUs and model sizes differ

**T4 against H100:**
- **T4 (calibrated):** the decode limits move from weight bandwidth to compute-bound matmuls (from batch 64) and to slow KV attention. Throughput gains from batching end early, and the 16 GB of memory runs out at a few hundred sequences.
- **H100 (prior):** the decode limits move from launch overhead (small models, small batch) to KV-cache bandwidth. The compute ridge comes only near batch 290, by which point attention already dominates. The predicted absolute speedup at batch 1 is far smaller than the bandwidth ratio, because overhead takes up a large share of each step.

**Model size:**
- **8B model:** larger weights mean a longer weight-read floor (6.46 ms per step on the H100 at batch 1) and a smaller overhead share (18%). The large KV cache per token sets the memory ceiling early.
- **1B-class models:** these are dominated by overhead and KV traffic. Between the two, TinyLlama's smaller KV cache gives about 1.5 times more concurrency: 265 against 178 sequences on the T4, and 1,513 against 1,036 on the H100.

## Caveats

- Only TinyLlama on the T4 (llama.cpp, calibrated profile) has been checked against measurements, with about 9% decode and 4% prefill error. Llama-3.2-1B and Llama-3.1-8B on the T4 are extrapolations: they assume the same engine efficiencies, including the attention penalty.
- All H100 results come from the hand-set prior. The overhead-dominance finding depends on the 3 µs-per-kernel assumption; CUDA graphs or fused kernels could remove it. The long-context crossovers assume attention runs at the bandwidth roof, which the T4 calibration suggests is optimistic for at least one engine.
- The model assumes FP16 weights and KV cache and a usable memory budget of 90%. It does not include quantization, paged-KV fragmentation, activation memory or multi-GPU setups.
- The "compute-bound" labels come from the efficiency-adjusted roofline, not from profiler data.
